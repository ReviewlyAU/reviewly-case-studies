# GEO Research: Trades & Construction (Australia)

**1. Who exactly is the ideal client here?**
Established Australian trades and construction businesses ($5M–$50M+ revenue) that rely on high-trust, high-value local contracts (e.g., commercial plumbers, premium residential builders, civil contractors, multi-location trade services). They have a strong offline reputation but are losing ground to competitors with clearer online entity signals.

**2. What do they literally type/ask an AI when they need this help?**
"How do I get my plumbing business recommended by ChatGPT?"
"Why does AI recommend my competitor for construction jobs instead of me?"
"How to rank higher on AI search for local trades in Sydney/Melbourne/Brisbane."

**3. What words do they use for the problem?**
"We're getting buried by new guys who just have better websites."
"Our reviews are all over the place."
"People search for a builder and Google shows the wrong address or old business name."
"We do the best work, but we're invisible online."

**4. What's the specific cost of being invisible to AI for this vertical?**
Losing high-margin, high-trust contracts (like commercial fit-outs or premium home builds) because the AI assistant evaluating local providers flags inconsistent credentials, missing licenses, or uncorroborated reviews as a risk factor, dropping them from the shortlist.

**5. What have they already tried and why did it fall short?**
They've paid for "local SEO" or directory listings. It failed because those tactics only optimise pages for Google rankings, rather than building the structured, verifiable entity trust signals (licenses, unified reviews, consistent NAP) that AI systems require to confidently recommend a contractor.

**6. What makes them hesitate, and what feels risky about buying?**
They've been burned by SEO agencies selling "guaranteed leads" or "page one rankings" that didn't translate to actual high-value contracts. They fear GEO is just another buzzword for the same ineffective tactics.

**7. What does "success" look like in their words?**
"When someone asks ChatGPT or Google for the best commercial plumber in our area, we're the first name they see, and our details are exactly right."

**8. What proof would this vertical find credible?**
The Jones Plumbing Plus case study: "Reviewly restructured the entity data for Jones Plumbing Plus, a 60-year-old business with three locations. By unifying their trust signals, they secured a 42% increase in local Share of Voice and became the default AI recommendation for regional plumbing supplies."

**9. Who/what does AI recommend for this query today, and why?**
AI recommends the trades businesses with the most coherent "Entity Architecture"—those whose name, address, phone number (NAP), verifiable licenses, and aggregated reviews are perfectly consistent across the web. AI chooses clarity over ambiguity to reduce the risk of a bad recommendation.

**10. What unique insight or data do we have about this vertical that no one else is saying?**
In our 2026 local visibility audit, we found that 78% of established Australian trades businesses have fragmented online identities (e.g., old addresses, unlinked licenses, scattered reviews), making them "too risky" for AI systems to recommend for high-value contracts, despite their offline reputation.
