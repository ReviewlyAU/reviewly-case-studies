The Challenge: Protecting an AI-Recommended Practice During Relocation

Gold Coast Chiropractic Centre is an established practice that Reviewly has partnered with for more than two years. Over that time, we built 30+ top-3 rankings across their Google Business Profile from 138 Smith Street, Southport — driven by geo-tagged photography, consistent patient reviews, and a local reputation that made them one of the most visible chiropractic practices on the Gold Coast.

The practice is owned by Dr. Alan Corin. Under his leadership, the practice has earned 152 Google reviews with a 4.9-star average — a rating that reflects not just clinical excellence but consistent patient experience, clear communication, and trustworthy care. This review volume and rating are not vanity metrics. They are AI trust signals that feed directly into how systems like Claude, ChatGPT, and Google AI Overviews evaluate and recommend businesses.

The practice operated from 138 Smith Street, Southport — a standalone address that was clearly, unambiguously theirs. Patients could find them easily. Google could verify their location without confusion. Their entity was simple: one business, one address, one identity.

Dr. Corin decided to relocate. The new premises offered advantages — better facilities, improved patient experience, a more modern environment. But the new address came with a fundamental visibility challenge:

The practice was moving to Unit 4/151 Smith Street, Southport — a first-floor suite inside a building shared with other allied health professionals.

This was not a simple address change. It was an entity complexity problem with stakes that extended beyond Google rankings into AI recommendation.

The Visibility Challenge: From 138 Smith St to Unit 4/151 Smith St

The new location created three specific risks:

1. Patient Confusion

A patient following Google Maps directions would arrive at 151 Smith Street. They would see the building entrance. They might see signage for a physiotherapist, a podiatrist, or another allied health practice on the ground floor. But Gold Coast Chiropractic Centre was in Unit 4, first floor. Without clear, prominent, and correct signage, the patient would be confused. They might enter the wrong practice. They might walk around the building looking for a door that was not obvious. They might give up and call — or worse, leave and go to a competitor.

2. Entity Fragmentation and Merging

Google's systems do not handle multi-business buildings well. When multiple businesses share one street address — 151 Smith Street — Google's algorithm must determine which business is which. It uses signage, website signals, citations, reviews, and user behaviour to make this determination. If the signals are weak or inconsistent, Google may merge the businesses, show the wrong business, reduce visibility, or trigger a manual review.

3. AI Signal Degradation

The 4.9-star rating and 152 reviews that made Gold Coast Chiropractic Centre a "standout" in Claude's recommendation are not static. They are living signals that depend on review velocity, review sentiment, entity consistency, and citation accuracy. A relocation that created confusion, negative reviews, or inconsistent citations would degrade these signals.

Left: The new front desk and building entry. Right: A treatment room in the new suite — the same clinical quality, new location.

The Solution: 6 Weeks of Systematic Preparation

Reviewly applied the REVIEW Method over a 6-week preparation period (May 2026). The approach was systematic, not reactive. Every action was designed to ensure that patients could find the practice, Google could verify it, and AI systems could continue recommending it — before the move happened on 13 June 2026.

Recognised: Making Unit 4/151 Smith Street Distinct and Findable

  • Signage strategy and execution: Designed, implemented, and executed a signage plan that made the practice visible and findable. This included building directory signage, suite signage, wayfinding support, and consistency with digital signals.
  • New geo-tagged photography: Professional photography of the new practice, building exterior, entrance, directory, and wayfinding to Unit 4. These images were geo-tagged with the new address coordinates and uploaded to Google Business Profile.
  • Website address updates: Updated the practice address across every page of the website. Ensured the address format was consistent and included the unit number clearly. Implemented LocalBusiness schema markup with the correct, detailed address.
  • Google Business Profile transition: Managed the GBP address change with careful timing. The new address was entered with the full unit and floor details. The GBP post announcing the move was published on 8 June 2026 — 5 days before the relocation.

Verified: Ensuring Citation Consistency

  • Citation audit: Identified every directory, health platform, and local listing source where the practice appeared.
  • Address update execution: Updated every citation with the new, detailed address, ensuring character-for-character consistency across all sources.
  • NAP verification: Verified that name, address, and phone number were identical across all updated citations. Confirmed no old address references remained.

GBP Relocation Announcement Post

The GBP announcement post published 8 June 2026 — 5 days before the physical move. Clear address, map, and patient communication in one post.

Established: Building New Trust Signals Before the Move

  • GBP post strategy: Published a series of GBP posts over the 6-week preparation period informing patients of the upcoming move and showcasing the new premises.
  • Website relocation announcement: Created a dedicated page announcing the relocation, with clear directions, parking information, and a map.
  • Review continuity: Encouraged patients to continue leaving reviews during the preparation period, ensuring that review velocity did not drop.
